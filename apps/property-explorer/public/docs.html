What Smart Site does

A smart site is everything you need to know about a place, in one place. Smart Site is the application that puts smart sites in your hands: the map, the layers, the parcel answers, the research, and the reports. It is an Empressa product operated by Legacy Group ATX LLC.

The information that governs a property is scattered across systems that do not talk to each other: the county GIS, the zoning ordinance, the FEMA panel, the code book. Answering one real question, what can I build here and what is the risk, means logging into all of them and hoping each is current. Smart Site assembles them against the place and answers the question, with the source, the date, and the confidence attached.

The map

The application is map-first. There are no pages to navigate; every tool opens over the map and closes back to it. It installs to a phone, so the answer is in hand while standing on the site.

The layers you can toggle:

  • Parcel boundaries
  • Zoning and land use
  • FEMA flood zone and regulatory floodway
  • Contours at 1-foot elevation
  • Hydrography, meaning streams and water features
  • Sidewalks and footpaths
  • Opportunity Zone tracts

One-tap presets bundle these for common reads: Default, Flood, which is parcels plus FEMA plus hydrography, Entitlement, which is parcels plus zoning, and Terrain, which is parcels plus contours. Map tools include measure for distance and area, draw and annotate, drop a marker, and jump to your location. Search understands parcels, addresses, streets and places, with type-ahead and recents.

Tap a parcel

Clicking any parcel opens a card with the core read: land use, zoning district, setbacks, the buildable envelope, flood status, and acreage. This card is free by rule. Whatever is in it costs nothing and needs no login.

Two things distinguish it from a map popup. The numbers are served from verified, precomputed records rather than fetched and hoped, and the envelope carries "approximate, not survey grade" honestly. And where a fact is not verified for that place, the card says exactly that, in plain words, naming what is missing. It never shows a guess.

The workbench

The deeper work lives in a small set of tools that open one at a time into a single dock beside the map. The map and the property stay the star; there is never a split screen and never a second permanent panel. Each tool remembers its state per property.

Property brief

The X-ray view of the active smart site. It leads with a verdict line, a plain-English glance composed from the verified facts, with red flags such as floodway placement leading when present. Below the verdict sits the cited detail.

Research chat

Property-anchored research. Every thread is tied to a smart site; there is no free-floating chatbot. Answers cite the property's actual records with numbered, tappable citations. Tapping one opens the evidence, the claim, the source, the confidence and the date, and lets you walk the reasoning chain in place: the envelope traces to the setback rule, the setback rule to the code section. The assistant can only cite records that exist; where the record is absent it says so. You can run several threads per property, attach documents as context, and pick a thread up later.

My properties, Compare and Share

Saved smart sites each hold their brief, drawings, notes and research. Compare puts two saved properties side by side. Share sends a link that carries your analysis, not just a pin: the recipient lands on the live map, flown to the property, with the analysis docked.

Reports

The report menu is X-ray, Flood and Drainage, Feasibility, and Comparison.

Two of them generate today. The Smart Site X-ray is the deep read on one place: what you can build with the envelope drawn, flood and drainage, terrain, codes and utilities, each layer sourced and dated, exported as a PDF with your notes and research summary and the site plan sheets appended. The Flood and Drainage study is a real computation rather than a lookup: the upstream catchment, the drainage zones where water concentrates, modeled ponding at design rainfall, and traced flow lines, rendered on the map over the property with a plain-language briefing and a formatted PDF.

Feasibility and Comparison do not generate today. They are on the menu and their generate paths are not live. Comparison as a side-by-side tool is live; the Comparison report is not. We would rather name that here than have you find it in the product.

Exports

  • Site plan. Layered CAD in DXF and IFC plus a PDF sheet: property lines, dimensions, setbacks drawn, contours, elevation labels, named streets, north arrow and scale, with a summary block and a provenance panel citing where every line came from.
  • Terrain. The site's terrain as a solid model in GLB, IFC or DXF, ready to drop into design tools.

Every sheet carries the same line: derived from public records, not a boundary survey, not for legal record. Verify with city staff and licensed professionals.

Records

Records is a request tool, not a report. It asks the county for the recorded documents behind a parcel and returns what it retrieves.

This is a real connection to county clerk systems, not a form that emails someone. Where a county's clerk portal permits automated search, Smart Site queries that portal directly. The terms of each portal are read and recorded before any request is allowed to run against it, and a portal whose terms do not permit automated search is not queried at all. That reading is a deliberate step, not an assumption: the permission is recorded per county, with the date it was reviewed.

What comes back is what the county actually holds. Where a document is not on file, or the portal answers with nothing, Records says so rather than returning an empty result that reads like an answer. Retrieval is also not instant: a request is a job, and the tool reports honestly while it is still running rather than implying it has finished.

Coverage

Coverage is nationwide United States, built jurisdiction by jurisdiction under mechanical certification gates and rolling out continuously. We do not publish a county list, because any list is stale the month after it is written and reads as a ceiling rather than a snapshot.

The safeguard is in the product rather than in a list: where a layer is not verified for your parcel, the product says so plainly, naming what is missing, instead of guessing. Coverage is a question the product answers about your parcel, not a question you have to ask us. Worth knowing too: much unincorporated land is legitimately unzoned, and an honest "no zoning applies" is the correct answer there rather than missing data.

How the system behaves when it does not know

  • Every substantive answer carries citations that open. A citation is something you can verify, not decoration.
  • Confidence is labeled with its basis and never dressed up. Where it is asserted from source quality rather than earned from outcomes, it says asserted.
  • Honest absence is styled distinctly and named specifically. The system telling you what it does not know is the reason to trust what it does.
  • Currency is checked rather than assumed. A repealed ordinance still published as current, or a re-platted parcel whose old record still resolves, is caught and said rather than served.
  • Sources found by web search, used as supplementary context, are visually distinct and labeled unverified. They never borrow the authority of a verified record.

The AI connector

Smart Site has two doors onto the same truth. A person opens the app; an AI client calls the same system directly through the Smart Site MCP server at mcp.smartsite.cloud. Same facts, same citations, same confidence, either door.

The connector is a door, not a plan. It is available at every rung including Free, it signs in against your ordinary Smart Site account, and it mirrors what your plan already allows in the app rather than carrying gates of its own. What a plan unlocks in the workbench it unlocks in the connector; nothing is moved onto or off the connector to make a plan more attractive. It is not the Hauska catalog at mcp.hauska.dev, which is a separate product.

Accounts and what is free

Browsing is anonymous and needs no account. A free account adds saved properties, three research messages per property, and sharing; the map, every layer toggle and the parcel card are free at that rung. The deep work, meaning the X-ray, the Flood and Drainage study, unlimited research, and the professional deliverables, sits behind a paid plan. The current plans and prices are in the app.

Sharing is free, and a shared property carries everything the sharer has stored at full fidelity regardless of the recipient's plan. What a plan gates is what you can produce on your own account, never what you can see of what someone shared with you.

What Smart Site is deliberately not

  • Not valuation. The product tells you what you can do with a place, not what it is worth. Constraints and buildability, cited, is the wedge; an opinion of value is out of scope on purpose.
  • Not comparable sales. There are no comps and no sold prices. Texas is a non-disclosure state, so sale prices are not public record, and the MLS vendor route is closed to a product like this one. Sales history carries dates and events honestly and marks the price absent rather than inventing one.
  • Not a listings site. It complements where listings live rather than competing with it.
  • Not survey or legal work. Nothing here approves, permits, or certifies anything.
  • No 3D. There is no 3D or tilted view in the product today, and we do not promise one.
